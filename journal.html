Notes on Fabric

Short, practical reading — not a textile course. Just the things worth knowing before you choose a shade.

Understanding Fabric

Why the Same Weight Feels Different

Two fabrics can carry the exact same weight on a scale and behave nothing alike on the body. Weight — measured in GSM, grams per square metre — tells you how heavy a cloth is. It tells you almost nothing about how it moves.

Drape is the separate variable, and it comes from how the yarn is spun and twisted, not from how much of it there is. A crepe weave with a tight twist will stand slightly away from the body no matter how light it's woven — that's what gives our Safa its dry, springy hand at just 150 GSM. A fabric engineered for fluid fall, like Noor, uses finer filament and a looser construction to fall close and move with light, even at a heavier 166 GSM.

The practical upshot: when you're choosing fabric, ask about drape separately from weight. "Heavy" doesn't mean "structured," and "light" doesn't mean "flimsy" — they're different questions with different answers.

Choosing by Season

Dressing for Lahore's Summers Without Giving Up Structure

The instinct in hot weather is to reach for the lightest thing available, and that instinct is usually right — up to a point. Below a certain weight, a fabric stops holding a line at all, and a suit that won't hold its line looks unfinished by the second hour, no matter how well it was cut.

The better question isn't "how light" but "how does it recover." A construction with good elastic recovery returns to plane on its own — no pressing needed between meetings. That's the specific problem our Safa code was built to solve: a micro-crepe weave light enough for eight months of heat, with a springy hand that keeps its shape instead of collapsing against the body.

If you're shopping for summer, ask to feel the fabric stand on its own for a moment before you ask about the weight number.

Fabric Care

Making Finished Suiting Last

Most suiting fabric fails early not from wear, but from care. A few habits make the real difference over a garment's life:

Hang, don't fold, between wears. A wooden or padded hanger at the shoulder line prevents the crease lines that folding sets into the fibre over time.

Air before you clean. A day on a hanger in open air resolves most odour and light creasing without a trip to the dry cleaner — over-cleaning is harder on fabric than a day's wear.

Steam over iron where possible. Direct heat and pressure from an iron can flatten a weave's texture permanently; steam relaxes the fibre without crushing it.

Confirm the shade before every cut. Dye lots vary slightly batch to batch — this is why we check every RAQI shade against the physical swatch at the appointment, not from a screen.
